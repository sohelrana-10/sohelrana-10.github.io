Thesis2026

DroughtNet: Satellite-Driven Drought Vulnerability Mapping for All 64 Districts of Bangladesh Using Machine Learning

Institution: Northern University Bangladesh

Summary

Drought is among the most pervasive climate hazards affecting agricultural productivity and food security in Bangladesh, particularly in the northwestern Barind Tract. Prior machine-learning studies of Bangladesh drought classification covered only 38 of the country's 64 districts, and their preprocessing pipeline had not previously been independently replicated. This paper makes three contributions: 1) an analysis of the most recent published baseline, highlighting methodological observations on data preprocessing and model evaluation, including variations in test-set composition across models (2) an alternative pipeline in which scaling and clustering are fitted exclusively on training data; and (3) an extension to all 64 districts with Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) classifiers, producing the nationwide ML-based drought exposure map for the country. The classical machine learning classifiers achieved test accuracies ranging from 85.92% to 90.31%. Among all evaluated models, the MLP achieved the best performance with an accuracy of 93.82% (weighted F1 = 93.81%), followed by the LSTM with an accuracy of 92.70%. SHAP explainability analysis agrees, in feature ranking, with temperature (T2M, TS) and surface soil moisture (GWETTOP) as the dominant drivers across MLP SHAP, RF SHAP, and RF Gini importance. The map identifies Chapainawabganj (35.42%) as most exposed and Sylhet (9.45%) as least, consistent with the documented Barind Tract drought zone.
